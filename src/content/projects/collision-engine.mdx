The floating glass cards on the home page aren't just decoration — asteroids can
actually hit them. Here's how that works without tanking the frame budget.

## Registering panels

Every glass card registers itself with a hook:

```ts
const cardRef = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
usePanelRegistration('hero-card', cardRef);
```

Registered elements land in a cached bounds map that is invalidated on resize
and shortly after scrolling stops, so we rarely call `getBoundingClientRect`.

## The check itself

Large moving objects become "colliders." A throttled loop runs a
circle-vs-axis-aligned-bounding-box test against each registered panel:

| Knob | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `colliderRatio` | Fraction of asteroids that can collide |
| `checkIntervalFrames` | How often the test runs |
| `minColliderSize` | Small rocks pass straight through |

When a hit lands, two things happen at once:

1. A **ripple** animates outward from the impact point on the panel.
2. The asteroid plays a brief absorption/shrink animation and despawns.

The geometry itself lives in a pure module with no DOM dependencies, which makes
it trivial to reason about — and easy to unit test in isolation.
